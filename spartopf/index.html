Privacy Policy

Ahmad Habboubah built Spartopf, a household budget book, as a free app for iPhone and Android. This SERVICE is provided at no cost and is intended for use as is.

This page explains how Spartopf handles information, since your privacy matters.

Summary

Spartopf does not collect, transmit, or store any of your data on any server. There is no backend and no account system, and you never log in to your bank. Everything you enter — months, income and expenses with their amounts, notes and due days, accounts and transfers, debts and loans, categories, budgets, savings goals, contracts, trips and projects, entries marked for your tax return, receipts and documents you attach, and your settings — is saved only on your device and never leaves it, unless you export or share it yourself.

Information Collection and Use

The app does not request, collect, or transmit any personally identifiable information. I (the developer) have no access to your data, no server it is sent to, and no way to view, recover, or delete it on your behalf — it exists only on your device. The only way I receive anything from you is if you write to me yourself (see Feedback and Ratings).

What the App Uses on Your Device

Some features use parts of your phone. Each one is used only when you start or switch on that feature, and none of them sends anything off your device.

  • Camera and photos. You can take a picture or pick a photo, screenshot or PDF to: take transactions from a photo of your banking app, attach a receipt or document to an entry, read the amount from a receipt, or scan an invoice's payment QR code (GiroCode) into an open bill. Text and QR codes are read on your device, by libraries built into the app. For transactions, amounts and QR codes, Spartopf does not keep the picture. An attached receipt is kept in Spartopf's private storage (not in your photo library): a photo is scaled down and saved again, which removes its location and other photo metadata; a PDF is kept as it is. On Android, the system's own camera and picker are used, so Spartopf does not ask for camera or storage permission.
  • Saving to your photos (iPhone). If you share the picture of your year in review and choose "Save Image", iOS asks whether Spartopf may add it to your photo library. Spartopf only adds that picture; it does not read your library.
  • Files you import. A bank CSV file, a bank statement PDF, an Excel workbook or a Spartopf backup that you pick is read on your device. You check the result before anything is taken over. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Face ID, Touch ID, fingerprint or passcode. If you turn on the lock, Spartopf asks the system (iOS or Android) to confirm that it's you, when you open the app and in the app switcher. The system only tells the app whether that worked; your face or fingerprint data and your passcode never reach the app. You can also hide amounts on screen.
  • Notifications. If you turn on reminders, Spartopf schedules local notifications on your device: before open bills are due, before a contract's last day to cancel, before a return window or warranty ends, and — if you turn it on — a daily reminder to enter your spending. There is no notification server. Notifications show no amounts unless you turn that on (the daily reminder never does), and they can appear on your lock screen.
  • Siri and Shortcuts (iPhone). Spartopf offers actions for Siri and the Shortcuts app: add an entry, ask for the month-end balance, list open bills. For them, the app keeps a short summary in a storage area on your iPhone that only Spartopf's own parts can read — never your whole budget book. Siri can say amounts aloud only while the app lock is off.
  • Automatic capture (iPhone, optional). If you set up an automation in the Shortcuts app yourself, iOS hands Spartopf the merchant, amount and card name of an Apple Pay payment, or the text of a notification from your banking app (iOS 27 or later). This happens on your iPhone. Spartopf reads the amount and payee from it to create the entry and does not keep the notification text. Nothing happens unless you create such an automation, and you can delete it at any time in the Shortcuts app.
  • Language, region and appearance. On first start, Spartopf reads your phone's language, region and light/dark setting to pick the app's language and currency. This stays on your device.

Where Your Data Is Stored

Your data is kept in Spartopf's private storage on your device, which other apps cannot read. On iPhone, these files are additionally protected so they can't be read while your phone is locked. Spartopf also keeps automatic backups there — one a day for the last 14 days and one a month for a year — so you can go back to an earlier version. Attached receipts are kept there too.

The setting "Include in iPhone backup" (on Android: "Include in device backup") is on by default. While it is on, your phone's own backup may include this storage: on iPhone your iCloud or computer backup, under Apple's privacy policy; on Android your Google backup and the transfer to a new device, under Google's privacy policy. If you switch it off, Spartopf moves your data to a place that is left out of these backups, and it then exists only on this device.

Third-Party Services

Spartopf does not integrate any third-party analytics, advertising, crash reporting or tracking SDKs, makes no network requests, and does not use cookies. Text recognition (tesseract.js), PDF reading (pdf.js) and QR code reading are open-source libraries that are part of the app and run entirely on your device. The Android app declares the internet permission only because its screens are loaded inside the app through a local address; Spartopf itself sends nothing over the internet.

Your device's operating system (iOS or Android) and the App Store or Google Play may collect standard installation and diagnostic information under their own privacy policies, which are outside this app's control:

  • Apple Privacy Policy
  • Google Privacy Policy

Exports and Sharing

Spartopf creates files only when you ask for them, on your device, and hands them to your system's share sheet — the file goes wherever you choose to save or send it (for example iCloud Drive, Google Drive, AirDrop or a message). This applies to "Export backup", "Export as CSV for Excel", the month report and year review as PDF, the tax list as PDF, the year in review as an image, and sharing an attached receipt. An exported backup contains your attached receipts and is encrypted with a password you choose, unless you switch that off; nobody, including me, can recover a forgotten password. A CSV file is not encrypted. PDF reports show amounts even when they are hidden in the app. Temporary copies made for sharing are deleted from the device afterwards. No data is ever sent to the developer, because there is no server.

Feedback and Ratings

In Settings → About Spartopf you can rate the app or send feedback. "Rate Spartopf" opens the App Store's or Google Play's own rating page; on iPhone, Apple's rating prompt may also appear now and then — Apple decides whether it shows. "Send feedback" opens an email to me in your own mail app, with only the app version and system version filled in. Nothing is sent unless you send that email yourself. If you do, I receive your email address and what you write, and use them only to answer you. You can ask me to delete your emails at any time.

Data Deletion

You can delete all data at any time in the app (Settings → Reset app). This removes your entries, attached receipts, the automatic backups on the device, the summary kept for Siri and Shortcuts, captured payments that have not been booked yet, and any scheduled reminders. Uninstalling the app does the same. Files you exported yourself stay where you saved them, and copies in your phone's own backup (iCloud, computer or Google) follow the rules of that backup. There is nothing stored elsewhere to delete.

Children's Privacy

This app does not knowingly collect any information from anyone, including children under 13, because it does not collect information from anyone at all.
